Privacy policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026. The French version is the reference text.

In short: Sommelle collects no data. Everything you enter stays on your phone. There is no account, no server, no advertising and no analytics.

What the app stores

To work, Sommelle stores on your phone, and only on your phone:

  • your answers to the starting questionnaire (age group and safety questions);
  • your sleep diary (bedtimes and rise times, estimates for the night, perceived quality);
  • your sleep schedule calculated each week and your rise time.

This information concerns your health. That is precisely why Sommelle never sends it off your phone.

What we receive

Nothing. Sommelle does not have permission to access the Internet: it technically cannot send anything. Nobody, not even the app's author, has access to your data.

Your phone's backup

Sommelle's data is excluded from Android backup: it is never copied to your Google account.

You can still move it from one phone to another, on your own initiative. When you set up a new Android phone (version 12 or later), you can copy your apps and their data directly from the old one, by cable or wirelessly: if you do not want that, untick Sommelle during the transfer. You can also export your sleep diary as CSV from the app, choose where to save the file, then import it again in the settings of the new phone. That file is a plain CSV, readable by anyone who opens it: store it like any health document.

Erasing your data

In Sommelle's settings, "Erase all my data" deletes everything from your phone. Uninstalling Sommelle also erases all its data.

The Sommelle website

This website is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies and no analytics. Like any host, GitHub may keep technical connection logs (IP address, date, time, page requested).

Your rights

Since no personal data is collected by Sommelle's author, there is no data on our side to access, correct or delete. For any question, you can write to pilmil.studio@proton.me.

Open code

Sommelle's code is public and open source: anyone can check all of the above by reading it on GitHub.

If you are going through a hard time, call your local emergency number or a crisis line in your country, such as 988 in the United States. In France, 3114 is free, day and night.
