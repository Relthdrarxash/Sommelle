Scientific dossier

This dossier describes what Sommelle does, rule by rule, and which guidelines each rule is based on. It is intended for doctors, psychologists, pharmacists and other health professionals who want to know exactly what the app offers their patients before recommending it, or who want to help us improve it.

Everything written here matches how the app actually works. The numerical values are gathered in a single place in the source code, which is public.

Status. Sommelle is a self-help and education tool, not a medical device. It makes no diagnosis, does not replace a consultation and claims no therapeutic effect of its own. Its effectiveness has not been evaluated in a clinical study.

1. CBT-I in brief

Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia in adults, ahead of hypnotics, according to European (2023) and American (2021) guidelines. It combines several components: sleep diary, sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive work and sleep education.

Digital versions of CBT-I are effective in trials, with smaller effects without human support. The main challenge is for the patient to complete the program: this is what guides Sommelle's design (quick entry, nothing that makes the patient feel guilty).

2. Program outline

WeekWhat the patient experiences
Before startingA 2 to 3 minute entry questionnaire (safety and profile), leading to one of the three pathways described in section 7.

1First sleep window (bedtime and rise time) and stimulus control instructions.
2 to 5Window adjusted each week; a new cognitive exercise unlocks each week.
6Before/after review.
After thatMaintenance: the patient can keep up the diary, and the window keeps adjusting.

A forgotten night can be filled in up to 48 hours later. There is no daily score, no streak to maintain and no red: a bad week is presented as information, never as a failure.

3. The sleep diary

The diary uses the Core version of the Consensus Sleep Diary (Carney et al., 2012). Each morning, the patient enters:

  • the time they went to bed and the time they turned off the light;
  • the time it took to fall asleep;
  • the number and total duration of night awakenings;
  • the time of final awakening and the time they got out of bed;
  • the perceived quality of the night.

Durations are estimated from simple choices ("less than 5 min", "15 min", "30 min"…), never timed: asking for exact minutes would push the patient to check the time at night, which maintains insomnia (Tang et al., 2007). An entry takes about twenty seconds. The app checks that times are consistent and flags inconsistencies without blocking the patient.

Calculations from the diary

  • Time in bed = out of bed − into bed.
  • Total sleep time = (final awakening − lights out) − sleep onset latency − wake after sleep onset.
  • Sleep efficiency = total sleep time ÷ time in bed.
  • Weekly efficiency = sum of sleep times ÷ sum of times in bed. Each night is therefore weighted by its length.

Time spent in bed before turning off the light counts towards time in bed but not towards sleep, which is consistent with the instruction to keep the bed for sleep.

4. The sleep window (sleep restriction)

The principle (Spielman et al., 1987): temporarily reduce time in bed to the time actually slept, to consolidate sleep, then gradually extend it.

RuleValue in Sommelle
Observation period before any restriction7 days
Logged nights requiredAt least 5 out of 7, otherwise the window is kept and the patient is told
Initial windowMean total sleep time during the observation week, rounded up to the next quarter hour
Minimum length5 h 30 min; 6 h for people at risk if drowsy; never below 5 h, whatever the setting
Maximum length9 h
AnchorFixed rise time, chosen by the patient, 7 days a week; the bedtime follows from it
Weekly adjustmentEfficiency ≥ 85%: +15 min; < 80%: −15 min (without going below the minimum); in between: unchanged
From age 65Thresholds lowered by 5 points (80% and 75%), as sleep is naturally more fragmented

Example

During the observation week, a 50-year-old patient spends on average 8 h 30 min in bed and sleeps 5 h 50 min: her efficiency is 69%. Her initial window is 6 h (5 h 50 min rounded up to the next quarter hour). She chooses to get up at 6:45 am: she will go to bed at 12:45 am.

The following week, she sleeps 88% of her time in bed. The window grows by 15 minutes: bedtime at 12:30 am. If her efficiency had stayed between 80 and 85%, the window would not have changed.

Precautions

Restriction causes transient daytime sleepiness, objectively measurable (Kyle et al., 2014). A warning about driving and dangerous activities is shown at the start of the program. Restriction is never offered in the situations listed in section 7.

5. Stimulus control

Stimulus control (Bootzin, 1972) aims to re-associate the bed with sleep. In Sommelle, it relies on three elements:

  • a fixed rise time, the same 7 days a week, even after a bad night;
  • a bedtime given by the sleep window, with an evening reminder that explains why;
  • an "I can't sleep" button that opens, at night, an almost black screen in warm colors and reduced brightness, with no time or numbers. It recalls the instruction: get up, go to another room, do something calm away from screens, go back to bed when sleep comes. It closes by itself after a minute and a half.

The other classic instructions (keep the bed for sleep, avoid naps or limit them to a short nap before 3 pm) are not yet presented in the app: they should be reinforced during consultations.

6. The cognitive exercises

Five short exercises (about five minutes each) unlock over the course of the program. They remain available once unlocked.

7. Safety and referral

The entry questionnaire leads to one of three pathways: full program, program without restriction (stimulus control, cognitive exercises, education), or referral to a doctor without a program.

Reported situationWhat the app does
Loud snoring with observed breathing pauses, or marked daytime sleepinessNo restriction; advice to see a doctor (possible sleep apnea)
Bipolar disorder, epilepsyNo restriction; advice to talk to the treating doctor
PregnancyNo restriction
Driving for work, dangerous machinery, safety-critical jobRestriction with the minimum raised to 6 h and a stronger warning, or no restriction, as the patient chooses
Very late schedule with normal sleep once asleepNo restriction; possible delayed sleep phase flagged and advice to see a doctor
Shift or night work, nights interrupted by someone else (infant, sick relative), under 18Outside the app's scope; referral
Distress expressedCrisis line highlighted (3114, the French suicide prevention line, always present in the app, with advice to contact a local crisis line elsewhere)

8. Follow-up: before/after review and summary for the doctor

Before/after review

From week 6, the app compares the observation week with the last 7 nights: sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, total sleep time, efficiency and perceived quality. The change is written out in words: "Improving", "Stable" or "Declining".

A question about daytime impact (from 0 to 4) is asked at the start and end of the program. It is not a validated scale. The Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) would be the reference tool, but it is subject to a license and is not included at this time.

Summary for the doctor

The patient can show you a summary directly on screen, or send it to you:

  • as a PDF: pathway followed, current window, week-by-week table, before/after review and details of the last 28 nights;
  • as a spreadsheet (CSV file, readable in Excel or LibreOffice): the full diary.

These files are created on the phone and it is the patient who chooses to share them (messaging, printing…). Sommelle has no server and sends nothing by itself.

9. What Sommelle does not do, and points to validate

  • It does not diagnose insomnia or any other sleep disorder.
  • It does not manage tapering or stopping hypnotics, which remains for the doctor to supervise.
  • It does not offer follow-up by a professional: it can, however, support existing follow-up through the summary.
  • It does not yet present all sleep hygiene and stimulus control instructions (see section 5).
  • Its own effectiveness has not been evaluated in a clinical study.

Several parameters are reasoned design choices rather than established values. We would like them to be reviewed by sleep professionals:

  • the minimum length of 5 h 30 min and the adjustment thresholds, especially the lowering from age 65;
  • calculating weekly efficiency on totals rather than as a mean of nights, and rounding the initial window up;
  • the maximum length of 9 h;
  • the content of the cognitive exercises and the order in which they unlock;
  • the daytime impact question, pending a validated scale;
  • the referral rules and the wording of messages, including the drowsiness warning and when it is shown.
